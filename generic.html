About Me :)

I grew up in California, surrounded by mountains, coastlines, and constant reminders of how alive the world is. As a kid, I was curious about everything — the patterns of ants on sidewalks, the colors of tide pools, the way small things fit into larger systems. That curiosity eventually led me to study biology, but it started much earlier, with long walks outside and questions I couldn’t stop asking.

Now I’m a PhD student in Organismic and Evolutionary Biology at Harvard University, where I study insect locomotion and collective behavior. I use computational tools and remote sensing to understand how animal movement reflects the environments they live in — how behavior, ecology, and evolution are connected across scales. What I love most about this work is that it blends observation and analysis; it lets me study life both up close and from above.

Before graduate school, I worked on projects that deepened my interest in the intersection of behavior and environment — from field research to data-driven studies. Those experiences taught me that science isn’t just about discovery; it’s also about care, creativity, and paying attention.

Outside of research, I like to spend my time cooking, hiking, and working on creative projects. I love sharing meals with friends, exploring new trails, and building or making things just for fun. I’ve always needed space for both focus and play — for the quiet patience of data and the messier joy of life outside of it.
